In context

How it plays out — for your scenario.

The same five capabilities. Different buyer situations. Here's what inqa looks like when it's actually running.

Scenario 01
Launching a new category
The product exists. The category doesn't yet.

You're not competing with an established alternative — you're asking buyers to see the world differently. The first question at every booth, after every ad, is some version of: "Wait — so what exactly is this?" The conversation that answers that question is the most important moment in your funnel. It happens hundreds of times a week, in places you're not.

inqa sits on your QR code, on your ad click, on your post-event follow-up link. When someone asks "how is this different from what I'm already doing?" — the conversation is trained to handle it precisely, in your voice, without a rep on the line.

What you get Across a week of conversations: the top "what is this?" framings your buyers use, which explanations land, which comparisons come up most. Your messaging team has a read on the category narrative — live, not in a quarterly survey.
Scenario 02
A complex consumer purchase
High consideration. Spec-heavy. Personal.

The buyer did their research on YouTube before they walked into your booth. They came back to your website at 11pm with six tabs open. They have specific questions — about compatibility, about learning curve, about whether this is right for someone at their level. No one in a general awareness channel can answer those questions in the moment they're being asked.

inqa is trained on the full product knowledge — specs, comparisons, use cases by skill level, common objections. It runs on the ad click, on the QR from the product card, on the post-interest follow-up. A buyer who lands at midnight gets the same considered, accurate conversation as one who calls a specialist at noon.

What you get Every spec question answered at the moment of peak consideration — not 48 hours later. Conversations that route to purchase, to a specialist, or to a nurture sequence based on where the buyer actually is — not based on which form they filled out.
Scenario 03
An established brand defending its innovation narrative
Known name. Misunderstood direction.

Your brand is known. That's the problem. Buyers think they already understand what you do — and what they think is last decade's version. Every conversation in the market starts from the wrong premise. Enterprise procurement teams compare you to legacy alternatives. Analysts use frameworks that don't fit your current product. The narrative about your innovation is being written by sources you don't control.

inqa is trained specifically on the new narrative — the contrast with the legacy position, the proof points on the innovation direction, the handling of FUD-style objections from procurement. It runs at every awareness touchpoint: events where your team is explaining the change, ad campaigns repositioning the brand, content targeted at technical evaluators.

What you get Every buying conversation starts from the right premise — not the legacy one. Objections about the old narrative are handled with evidence, consistently, across every surface. Your innovation story reaches the right evaluators at the moment they're forming opinions.
Scenario 04
Events — booth and conference
High cost. High moment. Short window.

You spent six figures to get to this conference. Your team had great conversations at the booth. Then the event ended. The badge scans went into a spreadsheet. The conversations that happened on the show floor — the specific questions, the real objections, the moments of genuine interest — disappeared. The follow-up email went to everyone equally, because no one knew the difference.

inqa runs at the booth via QR. The conversation starts when they scan — and continues after they leave. On the flight home, when they're actually thinking about it. In the week after, when the buying conversation with their team starts. The window that used to close when the conference ended now stays open.
