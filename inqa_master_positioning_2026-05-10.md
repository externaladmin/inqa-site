## 1. What inqa is

**Category:** The conversational layer for awareness.

**One-line description:** inqa is how marketing leaders stay in the conversations their buyers are having about their product — wherever those conversations happen.

**What it actually does:** Captures the moment of curiosity created by a marketer's channel investment (events, ads, social, content, conferences, shop floor) and turns it into a real-time conversation that is trained on the brand, qualifies fit, routes deliberately, and feeds aggregate intelligence back to the marketing team.

**What it is NOT:**
- Not a chatbot.
- Not a generic LLM.
- Not a CRM replacement.
- Not SaaS / not self-serve.

## 2. Audience

**Primary buyer:** Marketing leaders at companies launching something new — a new category, a new product, a new way of working.

**Roles:** CMO, VP Marketing, VP Brand, VP Demand Gen, Head of Growth, founder-as-marketer.

**ICP filter (internal use, never on website):** $5M+ revenue, meaningful awareness spend, marketing leader with budget authority.

**Reading register:** Lenny's Newsletter, First Round Review, occasional HBR. Follows Andy Raskin, April Dunford, Emily Kramer, Packy McCormick on LinkedIn.

**Three questions they ask on first read:**
1. Is this for me?
2. Is this real?
3. What does this cost me to find out more?

**Self-identifies through the "If you're..." portfolio** (see locked language below). Audience is intentionally broad — buyers self-deselect when they read the partnership model and realize this isn't a $99/mo SaaS.

**Real or aspirational fit companies (internal only, never on website without permission):** Range, DocuSign, Digits, ROLI, robotics company (unnamed), Ericsson, Nielsen Gracenote.

**Founder-led / thought-leader LinkedIn activity is a prospecting signal** (internal use only, not on website).

> *"Listens to what buyers say — explicit and inferred — and matches it to the right answer. Answers, educates, cross-sells."*

## 7. Insights / dashboard ("What you'll know")

Lives on the homepage between operational truths and partnership. Section title and lead:

> **Synthesized intelligence — across every conversation.**
> *Not a vanity dashboard. The kind of read on your market that lets you walk into a board meeting with answers — instead of guesses.*

### Six insight card categories

1. **Top questions this week** — list of top buyer questions with frequency counts
2. **Top objections** — list of objections with trend annotation (*"Brand voice up 60% from last month"*)
3. **Competitor mentions** — competitor names with delta (+/− %) and *"new"* flag
4. **Emerging segment** — big stat (*"+34%"*) + segment description + trend insight
5. **Conversion by channel** — list of channels with conversion %
6. **Brand voice consistency** — big stat (*"98%"*) + description

Example data in current artifact is illustrative; framing always positions this as *"what you might see."*

## 9. Voice and tone rules

- **Direct. Simple. Easy to grasp.** Goldfish memory in mind.
- **Slightly weary, peer-to-peer register.** Lenny / Packy McCormick / April Dunford voice.
- **Short sentences. Periods earn weight.**
- **Em-dashes** match the page register. Used throughout.
- **"Yours" / "you"** is the most direct possessive. Lands well.
- **"Brands" and "companies"** both work. *Brands* is more evocative; *companies* is broader.
- **Active voice**, with the marketing leader as the actor.
- **Prose like *"change how someone thinks about something"*** feels real. Avoid corporate.

### Allergic to (never use)

*AI-powered*, *leverage*, *unlock*, *supercharge*, *moments that matter*, *broadcast*, *empower*, *revolutionize*, *seamless*, *frictionless*, *cutting-edge*, *next-generation*, *transform your business*.

### Punctuation conventions

- Em-dashes (—) not en-dashes or hyphens
- Smart quotes (curly) not straight quotes
- Oxford comma in lists
- Period inside emphasized phrase (e.g., *"create awareness."* — period is part of the Coral phrase)

## 11. Engagement model (partnership, not SaaS)

This is a foundational positioning truth, not a sales objection.

**The claim:** inqa is not a SaaS subscription. AI agents that genuinely match a brand cannot be installed self-serve. They're tuned to the customer's goals, voice, and conversion patterns through partnership.

**The phases (internal language, not yet productized):**
1. **Setup** — initial training on brand, products, voice, fit criteria, routing rules
2. **Tuning** — first weeks of live conversation, iteration with the customer's team
3. **Optimization** — ongoing partnership against the customer's conversion goals

**Time-to-value claim:** Ready in weeks, not months.

**Pricing posture:** Not listed on website. Honest conversation, not a public tier table. The partnership framing filters out buyers who want $99/mo self-serve.

**Why this is correct positioning:**
- Higher LTV than self-serve SaaS
- Creates real switching costs
- Filters for serious customers (price-led shoppers self-deselect)
- Allows enterprise-grade pricing without enterprise-grade procurement
- Closer to Palantir / Scale AI / top creative agencies than HubSpot
